The full argument, in eight acts

We want to recover a function from finitely many noisy measurements. The naive approach — discretize first, place a diagonal prior on the coefficients, use the transpose as the adjoint — produces a posterior mean that looks fine. But the posterior samples reveal the truth: the prior has no continuous meaning, the variance diverges under refinement, and the adjoint ignores the geometry of the function space.

The cure is not a cleverer matrix. The cure is to choose the mathematical objects first — model space, data space, forward map, noise model, prior measure — and only then discretize. Each act below builds one piece of the argument.

Act I — A Familiar Problem. We set up a toy inverse problem, discretize immediately, and compute a posterior that looks reasonable.

Act II — Something Is Wrong. Sampling the posterior reveals trouble: variance grows with mesh refinement, and the posterior does not converge.

Act III — What Problem Are We Actually Solving?. The real problem is defined on a function space, not on a coefficient vector. We identify the five modelling choices that must be made before discretization.

Act IV — A Guided Detour Through Functional Analysis. From vector spaces to Hilbert spaces: norms, completeness, inner products, orthogonality, and why the choice of space matters.

Act V — The Data Space and the Adjoint. Finite-dimensional data, inner products, noise covariance, and why the adjoint is not just a transpose.

Act VI — The Prior Is Not Just a Matrix. Measure theory, Gaussian measures on Hilbert spaces, the trace-class condition, and how to build covariances from differential operators.

Act VII — The Repair. The corrected computation on the same toy problem: Bessel prior, geometry-aware adjoint, and why the pathologies melt away.

Act VIII — The Verdict. Same setup, two formulations, side by side. The full story from prior to posterior to refinement.

Summary & Appendices. The whole argument in one place, plus reference material on Gaussian conditioning, Hilbert-space measures, Gram matrices, and suggested exercises.
