Re-solving the Opening Example

The original naive workflow

We now return to the toy inverse problem from the beginning. The data equation was \(\obs = G m + \noise\), where the unknown model was a function on \(\Omega=[0,L]\), the data lived in \(\D=\R^K\), and the observational noise was Gaussian: \(\noise\sim\N(0,\Cd)\).

In Act I, we discretized immediately. The result seemed reasonable at first: the posterior mean looked acceptable. But Act II revealed the trouble. When we sampled the posterior, the uncertainty behaved badly under refinement.

The purpose of this final act is to solve the same problem again, but with the order corrected.

Basis first

The naive workflow began by choosing a mesh and hat functions \(\{\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_N\}\). The model was approximated by \(m_N(x) = \sum_{j=1}^N [\mathbf{u}]_j\phi_j(x)\). This step is not wrong by itself. The problem was that the basis became the starting point of the formulation. The unknown was no longer treated as a function \(m\in\M\). It was treated as a coefficient vector \(\mathbf{u}\in\R^N\).

Warning

A basis should represent the unknown after the model space is chosen. It should not choose the model space by stealth.

Diagonal covariance

After introducing coefficients, the naive workflow placed the prior \(\mathbf{u}\sim\N(0,\sigma^2 \mathbf{I}_N)\) directly on the coefficient vector. For fixed \(N\), this is a valid finite-dimensional Gaussian distribution. But as \(N\) changes, the prior changes too. These are not automatically approximations of a single probability measure on functions.

The trace of the prior covariance is \(\Tr(\sigma^2 \mathbf{I}_N) = \sigma^2 N\), so \(\Tr(\sigma^2 \mathbf{I}_N)\to\infty\) as \(N\to\infty\). This was the hidden reservoir of instability. Every mesh refinement introduced new independent variance.

Euclidean transpose

The naive workflow also used the transpose \(\Gb^{\T}\) as though it were automatically the adjoint. But the adjoint is defined by \(\inner{G m}{d}_{\D} = \inner{m}{G^{\adj} d}_{\M}\). With non-orthonormal hat functions, the discrete adjoint is \(G^{\adj}_{\mathrm{disc}} = \Mb^{-1}\Gb^{\T} \Wb_D\), not simply \(\Gb^{\T}\).

Unstable refinement

For \(\mathbf{C}_N=\sigma^2 \mathbf{I}_N\), the posterior covariance satisfies \(\Tr(\mathbf{C}_N^{\obs}) \geq \sigma^2(N-K)\), because the data can constrain at most \(K\) independent directions. As \(N\to\infty\) with \(K\) fixed, \(\Tr(\mathbf{C}_N^{\obs})\to\infty\). The data informed some directions, but the number of uninformed directions grew with the mesh. Each new direction carried variance of size \(\sigma^2\).

The posterior mean looked fine because means can hide variance. The samples showed the truth.

Lesson

The naive workflow did not approximate one continuous inverse problem. It created a new finite-dimensional Bayesian problem at every resolution.

The corrected workflow

We now rebuild the same example in the proper order.

Choose the model space

First choose the model space. For example, \(\M=L^2(\Omega)\) if we want square-integrable models, or \(\M=H^s(\Omega)\) if we want to encode a particular degree of Sobolev regularity. The important point is that the unknown is now a function-space object: \(m\in\M\).

Choose the data space

The data space is \(\D=\R^K\). We choose an inner product on \(\D\). In the simplest case, \(\inner{a}{b}_{\D}=a^{\top}b\). More generally, \(\inner{a}{b}_{\D}=a^{\top} \Wb_D b\). The natural data misfit is \(\norm{\obs-G m}_{\Cd^{-1}}^2 = (\obs-G m)^{\top} \Cd^{-1}(\obs-G m)\).

Choose the forward operator

Next define the forward map: \(G:\M\to\D\). For the toy problem, \([G m]_i = \int_\Omega K_i(x)\,m(x)\,dx\). This map is chosen before discretization. The adjoint is also defined at the function-space level: \(\inner{G m}{d}_{\D} = \inner{m}{G^{\adj} d}_{\M}\). Only after bases are chosen do we obtain the discrete representation \(G^{\adj}_{\mathrm{disc}} = \Mb^{-1}\Gb^{\T} \Wb_D\).

Choose the prior measure

Now choose the prior as a probability measure on \(\M\): \(m\sim\prior\). For a Gaussian prior, \(\prior=\N(m_0,C_0)\), where \(C_0:\M\to\M\) is a covariance operator satisfying \(\Tr(C_0)<\infty\).

A useful choice is a covariance built from a differential operator: \(C_0 = \alpha(\kappa^2 I-\Delta)^{-s}\). Here \(\alpha>0\) controls the overall variance scale, \(\kappa>0\) controls the correlation length scale, \(s>0\) controls smoothness, and the boundary conditions for \(\Delta\) encode boundary behaviour.

The trace condition \(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_j<\infty\) ensures that the prior has finite expected energy: \(\E\norm{m-m_0}_{\M}^2 = \Tr(C_0) < \infty\).

Only then discretize

Only now do we choose a mesh and basis: \(\M_N = \operatorname{span}\{\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_N\} \subset \M\). The coefficients \(\mathbf{u}\) are coordinates of a function in \(\M_N\). They are not the primary unknown. The primary unknown remains \(m\in\M\).

We discretize each continuous object: \(G \leadsto \Gb\), \(G^{\adj} \leadsto G^{\adj}_{\mathrm{disc}}\), \(C_0 \leadsto \mathbf{C}_{0,N}\). The discrete prior covariance is obtained by discretizing \(C_0\), not by choosing an unrelated diagonal matrix. The discrete posterior then approximates the continuous posterior \(\post=\N(m^{\obs},C^{\obs})\).

Lesson

In the corrected workflow, the mesh is a microscope. In the naive workflow, the mesh was the lawgiver.

Comparison

Posterior means

In both workflows, we can compute a posterior mean. The difference is not merely cosmetic. In the corrected formula, \(C_0\) is a covariance operator on the model space, \(G^{\adj}\) is determined by the inner products, the discretized covariance approximates the continuous covariance, and the posterior mean approximates a function-space posterior mean.

The two posterior means may look superficially similar in plots, especially at coarse resolution. But only the corrected mean belongs to a posterior measure with a coherent continuous limit.

Posterior samples

In the naive workflow, as \(N\) increases, new coefficient directions receive independent prior variance. Many of these directions are weakly constrained or invisible to the data. Consequently, samples can become increasingly oscillatory or unstable.

In the corrected workflow, the continuous covariance controls how much variance is available in each direction. High-index directions receive decaying variance. Refinement adds controlled detail rather than uncontrolled noise.

Posterior variance

The naive posterior covariance satisfies \(\Tr(\mathbf{C}_N^{\obs}) \geq \sigma^2(N-K)\), hence the total posterior variance diverges. In the corrected workflow, \(\Tr(C_0)<\infty\) and \(0\leq C^{\obs}\leq C_0\), so \(\Tr(C^{\obs})\leq\Tr(C_0)<\infty\).

This is the heart of the cure: finite trace \(\Rightarrow\) controlled uncertainty.

Mesh refinement

In the naive workflow, mesh refinement changes the prior: \(\N(0,\sigma^2 \mathbf{I}_N)\) changes with \(N\). Each new basis function receives variance \(\sigma^2\), so refinement injects new uncertainty.

In the corrected workflow, mesh refinement improves the approximation of fixed objects: \(\M_N\to\M\), \(\Gb_N\to G\), \(\mathbf{C}_{0,N}\to C_0\), \(\post_N\to\post\). The posterior at resolution \(N\) is no longer a separate model. It is an approximation to the same function-space posterior.

Intuition

In the naive workflow, refinement keeps building new rooms in the house and forgetting to install walls. In the corrected workflow, refinement sharpens the blueprint that was already there.

What the example teaches

The toy example teaches a simple lesson, but not a small one. A discretization can play two very different roles. It can approximate a continuous inverse problem that has already been formulated, or it can quietly define a finite-dimensional problem in place of the continuous one.

The same hat functions may appear in both workflows. The same forward matrix entries may be computed. The same data may be used. The same posterior mean formula may seem to appear. But the mathematical meaning is different.

The first workflow gives a sequence of finite-dimensional Bayesian problems. The second gives a sequence of approximations to one Bayesian inverse problem.

Checkpoint

The same numerical basis can either approximate a coherent function-space problem or define a sequence of unrelated finite-dimensional problems. The difference is the order in which the modelling choices are made.

Final summary

The opening mistake was not obvious because every finite-dimensional operation looked valid. The diagonal prior was a valid covariance matrix. The posterior mean was computable. The plot looked plausible.

But the posterior samples exposed the missing structure. The prior did not have a continuous meaning. The covariance did not have bounded trace. The adjoint was treated as a transpose. Refinement changed the problem rather than improving the approximation.

The corrected formulation fixes the order:

Think first, discretize later.

More explicitly:

  1. Choose the model space \(\M\).
  2. Choose the data space \(\D\).
  3. Define the forward map \(G:\M\to\D\).
  4. Specify the noise covariance \(\Cd\).
  5. Choose the prior measure \(\prior=\N(m_0,C_0)\).
  6. Define the posterior measure \(\post\).
  7. Discretize the already-defined objects.

Only then do the posterior mean, covariance, samples, and refinement behaviour belong to one coherent mathematical story.

Lesson

The final answer is not that matrices are bad. The final answer is that matrices should be shadows cast by mathematical objects, not the objects themselves.
