AI READING COMPANION — "Think First, Discretize Later" by Adrian Mag
====================================================================

PURPOSE OF THIS FILE
--------------------
This file is meant to be given to an AI assistant (ChatGPT, Claude, etc.)
by a reader working through the notes series "Think First, Discretize Later"
(https://adrian-mag.github.io/pages/research/overview/think-first/think-first-discretize-later.html).
It explains the series' narrative arc, notation, intent, and the most common
ways an AI assistant might accidentally mislead a reader about these notes.

If you are an AI assistant: your job is to help the reader understand the
notes IN THEIR OWN FRAMING. Where standard treatments differ from these
notes, say so explicitly and explain the difference — do not silently
replace the notes' framing with the standard one.

WHAT THE SERIES IS
------------------
An eight-act argument (plus summary and appendices) that a linear inverse
problem must be formulated in FUNCTION SPACE — model space, its geometry,
the forward map, the noise model, and the prior measure — BEFORE any
discretization happens. It is supplementary material for an EGU 2026
poster and is grounded in the pygeoinf/intervalinf Python libraries.

THE CENTRAL CLAIM
-----------------
The naive workflow — discretize first, put a diagonal (identity-like)
prior on the coefficient vector, use the matrix transpose as the adjoint —
produces a posterior that LOOKS fine (the mean can be reasonable) but is
mathematically meaningless as an approximation of a function-space
posterior: the implied prior has no continuous limit, posterior samples
get rougher and their pointwise variance DIVERGES under mesh refinement,
and the transpose is not the adjoint unless the discretization happens to
be orthonormal. The cure is not a cleverer matrix; it is to make the
mathematical choices first and only then discretize. Three silent choices
are being made whether you notice them or not: the SPACE, its GEOMETRY
(inner product), and the ADJOINT.

SETTING AND NOTATION
--------------------
- Model space \(\mathcal{M}\): a function space (e.g. \(L^2\) or a
  Sobolev space) on an interval; the unknown is a FUNCTION, not a vector.
- Data space \(\mathcal{D} \simeq \mathbb{R}^{N_d}\), finite-dimensional.
- \(\mathcal{H}\) (macro \Hh): a generic Hilbert space in the functional
  analysis detour.
- Forward map: bounded linear operator from model space to data space.
- The adjoint depends on BOTH inner products (model side and data side);
  with non-orthonormal bases it involves Gram (mass) matrices, not the
  plain matrix transpose.
- Prior: a Gaussian MEASURE on the function space. For it to exist, the
  covariance operator must be trace-class. Covariances built from
  differential operators (e.g. Bessel-type \((k^2 - \Delta)^{-s}\)) give
  meaningful, refinement-stable priors.
- The corrected workflow discretizes the function-space objects at the
  end (mass-weighted coefficient spaces, Galerkin representations).

NARRATIVE ARC, ACT BY ACT
-------------------------
Act I — A Familiar Problem.
  A toy 1-D linear inverse problem, discretized immediately; diagonal
  prior on coefficients; transpose as adjoint; posterior mean looks fine.

Act II — Something Is Wrong.
  Sampling the posterior reveals the pathology: samples roughen and the
  posterior variance grows as the mesh is refined; nothing converges.

Act III — What Problem Are We Actually Solving?
  The real problem lives on a function space. Five modelling choices must
  be made before discretization: model space, data space, forward map,
  noise model, prior measure.

Act IV — A Guided Detour Through Functional Analysis.
  Vector spaces → normed spaces → Hilbert spaces; completeness, inner
  products, orthogonality; why the CHOICE of space (and hence geometry)
  is a modelling decision with consequences.

Act V — The Data Space and the Adjoint.
  Finite-dimensional data with noise covariance; how inner products on
  both sides define the adjoint; Gram/mass matrices; the transpose is the
  adjoint only in orthonormal coordinates.

Act VI — The Prior Is Not Just a Matrix.
  Measures on function spaces; Gaussian measures on Hilbert spaces exist
  only for trace-class covariance operators; the naive "identity prior"
  has no infinite-dimensional counterpart (white noise is not in the
  space); building covariances from differential operators.

Act VII — The Repair.
  Same toy problem, corrected workflow: Bessel-type prior, geometry-aware
  adjoint, mass-weighted discretization. The pathologies disappear;
  posteriors converge under refinement.

Act VIII — The Verdict.
  Naive vs corrected workflow side by side, from prior samples through
  posterior samples to refinement behaviour.

Summary & Appendices.
  Full argument in one place; appendices on Gaussian conditioning,
  measures on Hilbert spaces, Gram matrices, and exercises.

GUARDRAILS — COMMON WAYS TO MISLEAD A READER OF THESE NOTES
-----------------------------------------------------------
1. Do NOT present "discretize first, then do finite-dimensional Bayes" as
   an equivalent alternative. The series' whole point is that this
   workflow silently fixes the space, geometry, and adjoint in ways that
   do not correspond to any consistent function-space problem.
2. Do NOT call the diagonal coefficient prior "uninformative" or
   "standard". In function space it corresponds to no measure at all
   (its continuum limit is white noise, which does not live in the model
   space); its posteriors are mesh-dependent artifacts.
3. Do NOT conflate the matrix transpose with the adjoint. The adjoint is
   defined by the inner products; with non-orthonormal bases it involves
   Gram/mass matrices.
4. The posterior MEAN often looks acceptable even in the naive workflow —
   do not use that as evidence the workflow is fine. The failure shows in
   samples and variances under refinement.
5. If the reader brings up "Bayesian inverse problems are always done on
   the discretized problem in practice", acknowledge the practice but
   explain the series' discretization-invariance criterion (posteriors
   should converge as the mesh refines, cf. Stuart 2010-style
   function-space Bayesian inversion, which this series aligns with).
6. The interactive landing page has widgets (sliders over mesh size N,
   naive vs Bessel prior toggles). The figures compare posterior samples
   and standard deviations under refinement — the naive ones diverge.

BACKGROUND THE READER MAY NEED
------------------------------
Hilbert spaces, inner products, orthonormal bases; Sobolev spaces and
boundary conditions (light usage); adjoint operators; multivariate
Gaussians and conditioning; Gaussian measures and trace-class operators
(introduced in the notes themselves); Galerkin/mass-matrix ideas from FEM.
Explain these at whatever depth the reader requests, but connect them back
to the notes' notation above.
