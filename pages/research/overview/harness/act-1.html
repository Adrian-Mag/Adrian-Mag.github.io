The Box Everyone Knows

Movement I · What you are actually talking to

Act 1 Current as of July 2026

You have used one. A box, a cursor, an answer. Perhaps it was useful. Perhaps it told you something false with complete confidence and you have not been back since. That failure is probably not evidence of what you think it is.

The interface is the same everywhere. One box. You type into it, an answer appears, and that is the whole of the machine as far as anyone can see. In one respect it is a remarkably honest interface: it hides nothing on purpose. For exactly that reason it is also deeply misleading. There is no visible difference between the box with almost nothing behind it and the box with a great deal.

Most people’s judgement of this technology was formed at that box. That is a reasonable place to form it. It is also a bit like judging an engine by driving the car around a car park.

Two bad experiences that look identical

Think about the last time one of these systems disappointed you. It was probably one of two failures, and they have almost nothing in common.

The first is the famous one. You asked for a fact and got a confident fabrication: a citation that does not exist, a function that was never in the library, a date off by three years. Everyone can name this one. It is a hallucination. It may never disappear entirely, though it has receded a long way in the past two years.

Then 241 + 241 becomes 483, which is 482 with the final 1 already folded in, and the 1 is then added a second time. Two errors, one cause. Nothing in the tone marks any of it.

That is worth sitting with, because it is not the failure the word “hallucination” usually conjures. Nothing was invented. The algebra was reasoned through correctly and stated correctly. What failed was the arithmetic, in the middle, silently, while the sentence around it stayed fluent. The prose does not slow down at the point of the error, because fluency and correctness are produced by the same process and neither one checks the other. Act 2 is about why that is the expected behaviour of the thing in the box rather than a bug in it.

The second is quieter, and I think far more common now. You asked something reasonable and got an answer that was not wrong, exactly, but was useless. Generic. Correct for someone, somewhere, in some situation, just not for you in yours. You wanted advice about your codebase and got advice about codebases. You wanted to know what a term means in your field and got the meaning it has in someone else’s.

The first failure is a defect in the model. The second is not a defect at all. It is the correct behaviour of a system that was never told which situation it was in, and had no way to find out.

The question this series is actually about

If you conclude from that second experience that the technology is oversold, you have drawn a conclusion about capability from an observation about context. The model answered the question it was given. The question it was given was missing almost everything that would have made a good answer possible: what you are working on, what you already tried, what your constraints are, what is in the file you are staring at, what your field means by that word.

None of that arrived, because nothing was there to bring it. The box does not know about your files. It cannot look anything up unless someone built it a way to look things up. It has no memory of yesterday unless someone gave it one. It has none of what a capable assistant would need, and the interface gives you no hint that this is what is missing.

So the interesting question is not how smart is the model? That question has a moving answer and everyone is already shouting about it. The interesting question is: what would you have to build around it before its answers were about your situation rather than a generic one?

That thing has a name. It is called a harness, and almost everything that changed in the past few years happened in it rather than in the model. People who get startling results are not, for the most part, using a different model from you. They are using a different machine around it.

To see why that machine is necessary, and why it takes the peculiar shape it does, you need to know exactly what sits at the centre of it. That is the next act. It is shorter than you might fear.
