The Machine Around the Model

The language model is the CPU. Almost everything that made it useful is the computer built around it.

Notes on AI in Practice

What this series is

If you have used a chatbot, been unimpressed, and concluded the technology is oversold — this series is written for you. That experience was real. But its usual cause has changed.

The models did get better; the era of confidently wrong arithmetic is behind us. Bad experiences did not disappear, though — they moved. The model can usually answer the question it was asked. The harder problems are whether it was the right question, and whether it arrived with enough of the situation attached to be answerable: the same question has many correct answers depending on context, and by default a model has almost none of it.

Expecting a bare model to handle a hard problem consistently is a mistake on our side of the screen. The capability has to be harnessed — and the machinery that harnesses it is what this series is about.

Eleven acts in three movements, from the chat box everyone knows to the machinery a user can actually build and change. Written by someone learning it while writing it, with working examples drawn from a real setup — and no examples invented for illustration.

Series in progress. Structure and navigation are in place; the acts are being written. Plan and verified sources live in docs/agent-docs/active-plans/.

Movement I · What you are actually talking to

Act 1 — The Box Everyone Knows. Start where the reader is: text in, text out, magic between. Name the common experience honestly — confident nonsense, and the conclusion that the technology is oversold. Promise: that experience was real, and it measured the scaffolding, not the intelligence.

Act 2 — Inside the Kernel. What an LLM is: a function from a token sequence to a distribution over next tokens. Name the two meanings of “layer” — transformer layers inside, harness layers outside — and then leave the inside alone. The model has no memory, no filesystem, no clock, no ability to act.

Act 3 — Even the Chat Box Is a Harness. The hinge of the series. A chat product is already several systems around the model. The reveal: the API is stateless — the whole conversation is re-sent every turn. “Memory” is an illusion assembled by software. Argued from necessity, not from undocumented internals.

Movement II · The harness

Act 4 — The CPU and the Computer. The metaphor formalised, then the mechanism: the model never executes anything. It emits a request; the harness executes and appends the result; the loop repeats. That loop is what “agent” means.

Act 5 — Anatomy of a Coding Agent. Concrete inventory of a large harness: tool suite, permission system, context management, subagents, session persistence. Same kernel, different machine. The capability gap is an engineering gap.

Movement III · What you can change

Act 6 — Steering by Text. The cheapest lever: a file the harness reads and injects into context. Not enforced — guidance, not law. Why that is both its weakness and its strength.

Act 7 — Packaging Expertise. Progressive disclosure: a short description sits in context permanently; the body loads on demand. Context is a scarce, paid resource, and everything loaded competes with everything else.

Act 8 — Mechanism, Not Trust. Where text stops being enough. Deterministic interception that runs whether or not the model cooperates. Includes the honest limitation: this is a blocklist, it over-blocks, and it has gaps. A mechanism without a stated threat model is marketing.

Act 9 — New Senses: How a Capability Becomes Available. Tool design. Definitions as interface contracts; why descriptions are load-bearing. The promote-to-a-dedicated-tool heuristic: gate, render, audit, parallelize. Closes with a short MCP section — host/client/server, and the one real invocation on record.

Act 10 — What It Knows. Context as the budget everything competes for. Retrieval as context assembly, not magic. Memory as files. Compaction, and why long sessions degrade.

Act 11 — Orchestration. When one loop is not enough: graphs, subagent delegation, who supplies the harness versus who supplies the deployment. With the counterweight — most tasks do not need this.

Closing

Summary — The Instrument. What the machinery is for: verification, audit, honest limits — and a frank account of where it is still bad.
