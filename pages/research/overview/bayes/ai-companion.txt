AI READING COMPANION — "Bayes, Measure-Theoretically" by Adrian Mag
===================================================================

PURPOSE OF THIS FILE
--------------------
This file is meant to be given to an AI assistant (ChatGPT, Claude, etc.)
by a reader working through the notes series "Bayes, Measure-Theoretically"
(https://adrian-mag.github.io/pages/research/overview/bayes/bayes-measure-theoretically.html).
It explains the series' narrative arc, notation, intent, and the most common
ways an AI assistant might accidentally mislead a reader about these notes.

If you are an AI assistant: your job is to help the reader understand the
notes IN THEIR OWN FRAMING. Where standard treatments differ, say so
explicitly and explain the difference — do not silently replace the notes'
framing with the standard one.

WHAT THE SERIES IS
------------------
A deliberately tiny discrete inverse problem — two hidden switches, one
lightbulb, one unreliable reporter — solved TWICE. First (Part 2) with
nothing but multiplication, addition, and division on a joint probability
table. Then (Parts 3a–3f) with the full measure-theoretic machinery:
sigma-algebras, measures, kernels, conditional expectation,
disintegration, and the posterior kernel. Part 4 places the two solutions
side by side with a notation dictionary. The intended reader has seen
Bayesian inversion before but is "still afraid of the measure-theoretic
sharks".

THE CENTRAL CLAIM
-----------------
The measure-theoretic formulation computes EXACTLY the same numbers as
the high-school table — that is the point. The machinery is not a
different theory; it is the same theory written in a language that
survives the passage to continuous and infinite-dimensional spaces, where
tables, densities, and pointwise conditioning break down. A second key
theme: the theory constructs the WHOLE update rule
\(d \mapsto \mathbb{P}(m \mid d)\) BEFORE any particular observation
arrives; the observed report is plugged in only at the end.

SETTING AND NOTATION
--------------------
- Model space \(\mathcal{M}\): four hidden switch configurations
  (finite set). Sigma-algebra \(\Sigma_{\mathcal{M}}\) (macro \SigmaM).
- Data space \(\mathcal{D}\): two possible reports. Sigma-algebra
  \(\Sigma_{\mathcal{D}}\) (macro \SigmaD).
- Prior measure \(\mu^0_{\mathcal{M}}\) (macro \muMzero) on the model
  space.
- Observation kernel: for each model m, a probability measure on reports
  — the unreliable reporter's behaviour (reports truth with probability
  \(p_{\mathrm{trust}}\), macro \ptrust). NOTE: these notes call it the
  "observation kernel" (earlier drafts said "likelihood kernel" — the
  current name is observation kernel).
- Joint measure on \(\mathcal{M} \times \mathcal{D}\), built from prior
  and observation kernel.
- Marginal/predictive measure \(\mu_{\mathcal{D}}\) (macro \muD) on data.
- Posterior kernel: for each possible report d, a probability measure on
  models — obtained by disintegrating the joint measure with respect to
  the data marginal.
- Observed report \(d_{\mathrm{obs}}\) (macro \dobs).

NARRATIVE ARC, PART BY PART
---------------------------
Part 1 — The Game.
  Setup: two hidden switches, a lightbulb (deterministic forward map),
  an unreliable reporter (noise), and a prior over configurations.

Part 2 — The High-School Solution.
  Build the joint table of (configuration, report) probabilities; column
  sums give predictive probabilities; normalizing each column gives the
  posterior update rule. Only arithmetic is used.

Part 3 has been split into six sub-parts:
Part 3a — From Points to Events.
  Why probability must be assigned to EVENTS (sets), not points;
  sigma-algebras; measurable spaces and measures.
Part 3b — Conditioning and Its Limits.
  Elementary conditioning P(A|B) and where it fails (conditioning on
  measure-zero events); why a better tool is needed.
Part 3c — Conditional Expectation.
  Conditional expectation as a measurable-function answer to "what do we
  expect given the information in a sigma-algebra".
Part 3d — Disintegration.
  Slicing a joint measure into a family of conditional measures over the
  values of one coordinate; existence and a.e.-uniqueness.
Part 3e — Bayesian Inversion.
  The Bayesian update as disintegration of the joint measure (prior ⊗
  observation kernel) with respect to the data marginal; the posterior
  kernel as the epistemological output.
Part 3f — Back to Finite Land.
  Descend from the general machinery back to the finite example and
  recover, number for number, the Part 2 table.

Part 4 — Summary & Notation Map.
  The two stories side by side; the final slogan; a dictionary between
  elementary and measure-theoretic notation.

GUARDRAILS — COMMON WAYS TO MISLEAD A READER OF THESE NOTES
-----------------------------------------------------------
1. Do NOT reach for probability DENSITIES and the familiar
   "posterior ∝ likelihood × prior" density formula as the primary
   explanation. The notes deliberately work with measures and kernels
   because densities need a dominating measure, which may not exist in
   the infinite-dimensional settings this series is preparing the reader
   for. Use densities only as a bridge, clearly labelled as such.
2. Do NOT say conditioning on a single observation point is trivially
   fine. Part 3b is precisely about the failure of naive conditioning on
   measure-zero events and why disintegration is the right replacement.
3. Keep the pre-observation/post-observation distinction sharp: the
   posterior KERNEL \(d \mapsto \mathbb{P}(\cdot \mid d)\) is constructed
   before data arrive; plugging in \(d_{\mathrm{obs}}\) is the last step.
4. The terminology here is "observation kernel" (not "likelihood
   kernel"); the object mapping data to posteriors is the "posterior
   kernel".
5. Both solutions give the SAME numbers. If your explanation of the
   measure-theoretic route produces different numbers than the table,
   something is wrong with the explanation, not the notes.
6. This series is finite/discrete on purpose. Resist "in the continuous
   case you'd just write an integral" — the continuous case is exactly
   where the elementary approach breaks, and the notes' machinery is the
   preparation for it.

BACKGROUND THE READER MAY NEED
------------------------------
Basic probability (joint, marginal, conditional); sets and functions;
comfort with abstract definitions. The measure theory itself (sigma-
algebras, measures, kernels, conditional expectation, disintegration) is
developed inside the notes — prefer the notes' own definitions and
notation when explaining.
