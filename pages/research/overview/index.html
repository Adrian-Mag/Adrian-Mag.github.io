Bayes, Measure-Theoretically

A simple discrete inverse problem, solved twice — first by tables, then by posterior kernels. Written for readers who have dipped their toes in Bayesian waters but are still wary of the measure-theoretical sharks lurking in the deep.

The example is deliberately small: four hidden states, two possible reports. We build the full posterior update rule before the observation arrives — the finite table is the baby version of the posterior kernel.

Part 1 — The Game. Part 2 — The High-School Solution. Part 3 — From Points to Events, Conditional Expectation, Disintegration, Bayesian Inversion, and Back to Finite Land. Part 4 — Summary & Notation Map.

Bayesian and Frequentist Inference

A conceptual comparison of Bayesian and frequentist inference from the same starting point — an observation equation and a noise model — showing how each constructs different epistemological objects around the same ontological situation.

The central message: Bayesian inference places probability on an epistemological representation of the truth; frequentist inference places probability on the observation procedure while keeping the truth fixed.

Part 1 — The Setup. Part 2 — The Observation Kernel. Part 3 — The Bayesian Path. Part 4 — The Frequentist Path. Part 5 — Beyond Additive Noise.

My Take on SOLA

A careful walk through the SOLA (Subtractive Optimally Localized Averages) method — from sensitivity kernels and resolving kernels to noise, proxy models, and the distinction between propagated noise and posterior uncertainty.

Ten acts (so far). The central message: SOLA reshuffles generative information — it does not create it.

Act 1 — The Kernel Game. Act 2 — Building SOLA. Act 3 — Averages Need Mass. Act 4 — Beyond Averages. Act 5 — Noise Enters. Act 6 — What Did SOLA Estimate? Act 7 — A Cautionary Tale. Act 8 — Inversion in Disguise. Act 9 — Uncertainty? Act 10 — Generative SOLA.
