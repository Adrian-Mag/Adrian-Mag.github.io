Physical-Property / Inference Graph

A prototype map of how geological state, rock properties and geophysical observations connect, and of which way each connection can be run.

Interactive tool · prototype

State, properties, observations, and the arrows between them

Every geophysical inference is a chain: a geological state (temperature, porosity, fluid content, mineralogy) sets rock properties (velocities, density, resistivity), and the properties set what an instrument can see (travel times, gravity anomalies, apparent resistivity). The graph writes that chain down explicitly. Each arrow is a physical relation with its equation, its references and, separately, a statement of whether the arrow can be run backwards: uniquely, up to a family of solutions, or not at all without a prior.

Under construction This is an early prototype that I am actively building. The set of quantities and relations is incomplete, some entries are still being checked against the literature, and the layout and wording change often. Treat it as a sketch to discuss, not as a reference. Feedback is welcome.

How to read it

  • Quantities are the nodes, arranged in layers from geological state at the top to observations at the bottom.
  • An arrow is a relation. When a relation has several inputs (Gassmann, Archie, the Poisson ratio from VP and VS), the inputs meet at a small diamond, the relation junction, before the arrow continues to the output.
  • Click a node, an arrow or a diamond: the card on the right shows the equation, the free parameters, the evidence type and the references.
  • Forward mode colours arrows by the kind of relation; inference mode recolours them by how well the relation can be inverted.

Line styles

  • Solid: an explicit or bijective formula.
  • Dashed: an implicit or constitutive model with free parameters.
  • Dotted: an empirical or probabilistic relation.
  • Thick: an observation operator, from a property field to a measurable.

The "connection" tool in the sidebar finds a chain between two quantities and lists, arrow by arrow, what else each step needs. In inference mode it gives a verdict for the chain: which steps are unique, which are ill-posed, and which need a prior.
